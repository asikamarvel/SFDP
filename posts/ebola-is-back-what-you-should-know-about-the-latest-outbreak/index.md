For decades, Ebola virus disease has remained one of the world’s most feared infectious diseases. It is k nown for causing severe illness and deadly outbreaks and continues to raise concern whenever new cases emerge. In recent weeks, global attention has again turned toward Ebola following a new outbreak involving the Bundibugyo strain in parts of the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Uganda. As of May 24, the Ministries of Health in the DRC and Uganda have reported the following: DRC: A total of 904 suspected cases, 101 confirmed cases, 119 suspected deaths, and 10 confirmed deaths. Uganda : A total of 5 confirmed cases and 1 confirmed death. Health authorities are working to contain the spread , while researchers and public health experts closely monitor the situation. What Is Ebola? Ebola virus disease (EVD) is a severe viral illness that affects humans and some animals, particularly primates. The disease is caused by viruses in the Ebolavirus genus . It spreads through direct contact with infected bodily fluids , such as blood, saliva, sweat, vomit, or urine, or through contaminated surfaces. Ebola outbreaks have recorded fatality rates ranging from 25% to 90%, depending on the strain, healthcare response, and speed of detection. The First Ebola Outbreak in History Ebola was first identified in 1976 during two simultaneous outbreaks in the p resent-day Democratic Republic of Congo and Sudan . The disease was named after the Ebola River near one of the outbreak locations in Congo.

The first outbreaks were devastating because healthcare workers had little understanding of the virus at the time. The Different Strains of Ebola Not all Ebola viruses are the same. Scientists have identified several species of the virus, including: Zaire Ebola Virus (EBOV) This is the deadliest and most common strain. It caused the massive 2014–2016 West Africa outbreak, the largest Ebola outbreak ever recorded . Currently, it is the only variant with highly effective licensed vaccines ( such as Ervebo) . Sudan Virus (SUDV) This strain has led to several outbreaks in Uganda and Sudan. It is highly lethal, resulting in death in approximately 50% of those infected. Bundibugyo Virus (BDBV) This is the strain linked to the current outbreak in DRC and Uganda. It was first identified in western Uganda in 2007. There is currently no licensed vaccine specifically approved for this strain. Taï Forest Virus (TAFV) It is a rare strain first identified in chimpanzees in Taï National Park, Côte d&apos;Ivoire, in 1994.

Reston Virus

(RESTV) This strain is f ound mainly in animals and has not been known to cause severe disease in humans. Bombali ebolavirus (BOMV) Discovered in 2018 in bats in Sierra Leone, this is the most recently named species. It has also been detected in bats in other African countries , such as Guinea, Kenya, and Mozambique. T here are no known human cases or outbreaks caused by the Bombali virus .
